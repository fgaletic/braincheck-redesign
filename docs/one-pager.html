What I explored, and why

  • Status is never colour alone. Valid, Incomplete and Invalid are always icon plus text.
  • Glove-friendly. 58 px buttons and 56 px fields, one task per screen.
  • The timer is the main object. The IFU has two thresholds and an expiry; the ring, colour and a 0 / 15 / 20 minute timeline make them visible at a glance.
  • No interpretation. Results show where a value sits in the measuring range, with "<2000" and ">120" shown as the IFU specifies. No normal or abnormal labels.
  • Deviations are visible. A skipped timer needs a confirmation and is flagged on the result.
  • Scan conditions at the point of need. Surface, shadows and steadiness are text in the IFU; here they sit on the scan screen.
Try it fast: a prototype control speeds the timer up (30× / 60×) and picks the simulated outcome (valid, out of range, incomplete, invalid).

Engineering

  • Expo 58, React Native 0.88, strict TypeScript. Runs on web, iOS and Android; checked on web.
  • Pure domain layer for timer phases, classification, range display and retention, with no UI imports.
  • 23 unit tests and a CI workflow (typecheck + tests).
  • Traceability table in the README: requirement IDs mapped to IFU sections and tests.
  • Typed i18n dictionary: a missing Spanish key fails the build.
